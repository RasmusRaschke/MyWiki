# Free Abelian Groups

``` ad-Definition
title: Definition (Free Resolution).
Let $A$ be an abelian group. A <ins>free resolution</ins> of $A$ is a [[short exact sequence]]
$$
0 \to R \to \cF \to A \to 0
$$
such that $\cF$ is a free abelian group.
```

**Example.**
For all $n \geq 1$, the [[short exact sequence]]
```tikz
\usepackage{tikz-cd}
\usepackage{amsmath, amstext, amssymb, amsfonts}
\newcommand{\Z}{\mathbb{Z}}
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzcd}[scale=3]
0 \ar[r] & \Z \ar[r, "\cdot n"] & \Z \ar[r, "\pi"] & \Z / n\Z \ar[r]& 0 
\end{tikzcd}
\end{document}
```
is a free resolution of $\Z / n\Z.$

## Standard Resolution

``` ad-Theorem
title: Theorem (Standard Resolution).
Every abelian group has a free resolution, called <ins>standard resolution</ins>.

```
*Proof.*
We construct this resolution explicitly. Let $A$ be any abelian group and $\cF$ be the free abelian group generated by the elements of $A$ as a [[set]]. Denote by $y_a$ the basis element of $\cF$ corresponding to $a \in A$. We define a homomorphism
$$
\begin{align}
p: \cF &\to A\\ \\
\sum_{a \in A} \lambda_a y_a &\mapsto p \left( \sum_{a \in A} \lambda_a y_a\right) := \sum_{a \in A} \lambda_a a.
\end{align}
$$
In this sum, the integers $\lambda_a \in \Z$ are only non-trivial for finitely many $a \in A$. This map is clearly an [[epimorphism]]. Defining $R:=\ker(p)$, we obtain the desired free resolution by construction.
<span style="float:right;">$\blacksquare$</span>
