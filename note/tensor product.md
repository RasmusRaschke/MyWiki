# Universal Property

``` ad-Definition
title: Definition (Tensor Product).
Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $M, N$ be $R$-modules. The <ins>tensor product</ins> $M \otimes_R N$ of $M$ and $N$ over $R$ is an $R$-module satisfying the following [[universal properties|universal property]]:
For any $R$-bilinear map $\phi: M \times N \to P$ there is an $R$-bilinear map
$$
\otimes: M \times N \to M \otimes_R N
$$
such that there exists an $R$-module homomorphism $\overline{\phi}: M \otimes_R N \to P$ unique up to isomorphism with
$$
\phi = \overline{\phi} \circ \otimes.
$$

```
This is equivalent to the commutativity of
```tikz
\usepackage{tikz-cd}
\usepackage{amsmath, amstext, amssymb, amsfonts}
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzcd}[scale=3]
M \times N \ar[d, "\otimes"] \ar[r, "\phi"] & P \\
M \otimes_R N \ar[ur, "\exists ! \overline{\phi}"'] &.
\end{tikzcd}
\end{document}
```

# Abelian Groups

 ``` ad-Definition
title: Definition (Tensor Product of Abelian Groups).
The <ins>tensor product</ins> $A \otimes B$ of two abelian groups $A$ and $B$ is the [[relation#Equivalence Relation|quotient]] of the free abelian group generated by $A \times B$ (as a set) under the relations
$$
(a_1+a_2,b) \sim (a_1,b)+(a_2,b)
$$
and
$$
(a, b_1+b_2) \sim (a,b_1)+(a,b_2)
$$
for $a_1,a_2,a \in A$ and $b_1,b_2,b \in B$. Equivalence classes are denoted by $a \otimes b \in A \otimes B$.

```

**Remarks.**
1. The tensor product satisfies the [[universal properties|universal property]] described above.
2. The relations imply $\lambda (a \otimes b) = (\lambda a) \otimes b = a \otimes (\lambda b)$ for any $\lambda \in \Z$. Elements of the tensor product are finite linear combinations $$\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i (a_i \otimes b_i).$$ Elements of the form $a \otimes b$ with $a \in A, b \in B$ generate $A \otimes B$ and are called <ins>pure tensors</ins>.
3. The tensor product is symmetric up to isomorphism with the symmetry [[isomorphism]] given by $$\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i (a_i \otimes b_i) \mapsto \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i (b_i \otimes a_i).$$ Similarly, the tensor product is associative up to isomorphism.
4. Consider $A \otimes \Z:$ The identity $$\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i (a_i \otimes \mu_i) = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i(\mu_i \otimes a_i) = \sum_{i=1}^n (\lambda_i \mu_i) (1 \otimes a_i)$$ yields $$A \otimes \Z \cong A \cong \Z \otimes A$$ for any abelian group $A$.
5. Furthermore, we have $A \otimes \Z / n\Z \cong A / nA$ with $nA:=\{na|a \in A\}$ for any abelian group $A$. The [[isomorphism]] is just reduction: $$ a \otimes [k]_n \mapsto [a k]_n.$$
6. Let $f \in \Hom(A, A')$ and $g \in \Hom(B, B')$. The $\otimes$ functor induces a homomorphism $$f \otimes g: A \otimes B \to A' \otimes B'$$ given by $$(f \otimes g)(a \otimes b) = f(a)\otimes g(b).$$

## Tensor is not an exact functor

Let 
```tikz
\usepackage{tikz-cd}
\usepackage{amsmath, amstext, amssymb, amsfonts}
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzcd}[scale=3]
0 \ar[r] & A \ar[r, "\alpha"] & B \ar[r, "\beta"] & C \ar[r] & 0
\end{tikzcd}
\end{document}
```
be a [[short exact sequence|short exact sequence]] of abelian groups. In general, the functor $\cdot \otimes D$ for some abelian group $D$ fails to be right-exact. For example, take
```tikz
\usepackage{tikz-cd}
\usepackage{amsmath, amstext, amssymb, amsfonts}
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzcd}[scale=3]
0 \ar[r] & \mathbb{Z} \ar[r] & \mathbb{Q} \ar[r] & \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \ar[r] & 0
\end{tikzcd}
\end{document}
```
which is obviously exact. However, 
```tikz
\usepackage{tikz-cd}
\usepackage{amsmath, amstext, amssymb, amsfonts}
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzcd}[scale=3]
0 \ar[r] & \mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \ar[r] & \mathbb{Q} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \ar[r] & \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \ar[r] & 0
\end{tikzcd}
\end{document}
```
fails to be exact as every rational number $\frac{p}{q}, \, q \neq 0$ can be written as $2\frac{p}{2q}$ and hence $\Q \otimes \Z / 2\Z \cong \Q / 2 \Q \cong 0$. But we have:
``` ad-Proposition
title: Proposition (Right-Exactness of the $\otimes$-Functor).
For every abelian group $D$, the [[functor]] $(\cdot) \otimes D$ is right-exact. If the original [[short exact sequence]] splits, so does its image under $(\cdot) \otimes D$.

```
